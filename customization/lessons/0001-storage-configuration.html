Storage Configuration: NAS

Central storage on the network, and the disk choice that saves energy.

The win

By the end of this lesson you'll know a NAS is the scalable central storage solution (Q42), and lower rotational speed disks cut energy use (Q41).

What a NAS is

A network-attached storage (NAS) device is dedicated storage connected to the network, shared by many users. Because it's a central, expandable appliance, it automatically scales with increasing storage needs — add drives or bays as demand grows.

Energy: rotational speed

Spinning disks consume energy in proportion to how fast they spin. For a video company that wants massive storage but minimal energy usage, the answer is to install lower rotational speed disks — they turn more slowly and draw less power.

ChoiceEffect on energy
Lower rotational speed disksLess energy (slower spin = less power)
Filling every drive bayMore drives = more energy
Adding more fansMore energy (extra cooling)
Placement near computersNo meaningful energy change

The trap

For "massive storage + minimize energy," the winning lever is lower rotational speed. Filling bays and adding fans both increase energy use, and moving the NAS doesn't help.
