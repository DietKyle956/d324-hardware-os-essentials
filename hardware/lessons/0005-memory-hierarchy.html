The Memory Hierarchy: ROM, Cache, RAM, Virtual RAM

One ladder of memory, from the tiny-and-fast to the large-and-slow. Learn it once and three exam questions (Q12, Q13, Q14) answer themselves.

The win

By the end of this lesson you'll know the speed/size ladder cold — including the one you missed: L1 cache is the smallest but fastest (Q13).

The speed/size ladder

Memory trades off speed against size. The closer a memory sits to the CPU, the faster it is — and the smaller it has to be.

The three answers you need

MemoryKey factExam question
ROMRead-only memory — holds firmware/BIOS and does not change during the computer's life span (non-volatile)Q12
L1 cacheSmallest but fastest — sits closest to the core; L2 and L3 grow larger and slower in orderQ13
Virtual RAMStored on a drive as a paging file; extends physical RAM when it runs lowQ14

Two distinctions that carry the rest

Volatile vs non-volatile. RAM and cache are volatile — they lose their data the instant power is cut. ROM is non-volatile — it keeps its contents without power, which is why it can hold the firmware that boots the machine.

Cache beats RAM. The whole point of L1/L2/L3 cache is that it's faster than main RAM — that's why the CPU checks cache first. Virtual RAM is the opposite trick: when RAM is full, the OS swaps pages out to the drive, trading speed for capacity.
