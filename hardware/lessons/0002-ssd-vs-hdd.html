SSD vs HDD: The Trade-Offs

Two ways to store the same bits — one mechanical, one electronic. Learn which one wins on speed, cost, capacity, and endurance so you can answer any "advantage of" question cold.

The win

By the end of this lesson you'll be able to answer any "what's an advantage of SSD vs HDD?" question by remembering a single fact — SSDs have no moving parts — and deriving the rest of the trade-offs from it.

What's different under the hood

An HDD is the mechanical device from Lesson 1: spinning platters and an actuator arm that physically moves the read/write heads to where the data lives. An SSD has no platters and no heads — data lives in NAND flash memory, read and written purely electronically.

The trade-off matrix

Nearly every exam question about SSD vs HDD reduces to this table:

FactorSSDHDD
Data transfer speedWins — no seek timeSlow — head must physically move
Cost per byteHigherWins — cheaper
Capacity per dollarLowerWins — more storage for the money
Shock / durabilityWins — no moving parts to breakFragile — a drop can crash the heads
Write enduranceFinite — flash cells wear outWins — rewritable far longer
Power / heat / noiseWins — less of all threeMore of all three

The trap

"Unlimited write operations" is never the right answer for SSD. Flash cells physically wear out after a finite number of write/erase cycles. Neither drive is unlimited — but SSDs are the one with a well-known write-endurance limit.
