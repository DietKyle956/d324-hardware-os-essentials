Thermal Troubleshooting: Overheating & Shutdowns

The last Hardware lesson. When a machine overheats and shuts down, the fix is almost always airflow — not the battery, RAM, or OS.

The win

By the end of this lesson you'll map the symptom (overheating + unexpected shutdown) straight to the right fix (clean the vents and fan), and you'll know why the other options don't address heat.

The symptom → cause → fix chain

A processor generates heat, and a heatsink + fan carries it away through the vents. When dust clogs the vents or the fan, heat has nowhere to go. The CPU hits its temperature limit and the system throttles or shuts down to protect itself from damage.

Why the other answers miss

The exam's distractors each fix a different problem — just not heat:

OptionWhat it actually fixes
Replace the batteryPower / charging problems — not heat
Upgrade the RAMMemory capacity — not heat
Reinstall the operating systemSoftware corruption — not heat

Troubleshooting rule

Overheating + shutdown = thermal → check airflow first. Clean the vents and fan with compressed air (cheapest, most common cause), then consider a failed fan or dried-out thermal paste if it persists.
