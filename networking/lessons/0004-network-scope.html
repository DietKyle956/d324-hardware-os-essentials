Network Scope: PAN, LAN, MAN, WAN

Four sizes of network. Match the geography to the acronym.

The win

By the end of this lesson you'll know a single large building = LAN (Q36), and how PAN, MAN, and WAN sit around it on the size ladder.

The size ladder

TypeFull nameSpanExample
PANPersonal area network~10 m around one personPhone to earbuds (Bluetooth)
LANLocal area networkA single building or siteOffice, school
MANMetropolitan area networkA city or metro regionCity-wide network
WANWide area networkAcross regions or countriesThe internet

A LAN connects computers and devices within a single building or campus — one office, one school, one large building. When the question says "a single large building," it's describing a local area network.

Going up: a MAN covers a city, and a WAN spans regions or the globe. Going down: a PAN is just the few meters around one person.

The trap

"Single large building" = LAN, not MAN (city) or WAN (regions/countries). Anchor the ladder by size: Personal (~10 m) < Local (building) < Metropolitan (city) < Wide (world).
