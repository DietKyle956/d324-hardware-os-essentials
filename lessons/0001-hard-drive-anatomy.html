Hard Drive Anatomy: Where Data Actually Lives

One small lesson that settles the question you missed — and gives you a mental model for every storage question that follows.

The win By the end of this lesson you'll be able to point at any hard-disk diagram and say, without hesitation, which part stores the data, which part reads it, and how the disk is organized. This is exactly the question you missed on the pre-assessment (Q6).

The four parts that get confused

PartWhat it isWhat it does
PlattersRigid circular disks coated in magnetic materialStore the actual data as magnetic patterns
Read/write headsTiny electromagnetic devices on an actuator armRead and write the magnetic bits; they never touch the surface
TracksConcentric circles on a platterWhere data is laid out — an addressing scheme, not a storage medium
SectorsPie-shaped slices of a trackThe smallest addressable unit of storage
The one distinction that matters Platters are the medium; tracks and sectors are the map. The platter is the physical thing that holds your data. Tracks and sectors just describe where on the platter to look. The read/write heads do the actual reading and writing.
