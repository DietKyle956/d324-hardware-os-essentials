Capacity Planning: RAM Arithmetic

Simple arithmetic with a twist: always account for the OS, and round down when dividing.

The win By the end of this lesson you'll solve RAM capacity problems the way the OA frames them: add up all needs, and subtract the OS before dividing.

Two patterns

Pattern 1 — minimum total: add every need together, including the OS.

Pattern 2 — maximum instances: subtract the OS's share first, then divide what's left by each instance's need, and round down (you can't run a fraction of an instance).

Worked examples Minimum: two apps need 4 GB each (8 GB) and the OS needs 4 GB → 4 + 4 + 4 = 12 GB.
Maximum: 32 GB total, OS needs 2 GB → 30 GB left. Each instance needs 7 GB → 30 ÷ 7 = 4.28 → 4 instances (round down).
