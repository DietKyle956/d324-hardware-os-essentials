Social Engineering and Related Threats

The human attack. Know what social engineering is — and what it isn't.

The win By the end of this lesson you'll know social engineering = acquiring information through personal interaction (Q40), and how to separate it from a botnet, a threat agent, and an antivirus engine.

The human factor

Social engineering attacks the person, not the machine. The attacker manipulates someone — through conversation, a convincing phone call, a fake email, or a friendly "tailgate" through a door — to acquire information about a network or system through personal interactions.

Common forms include phishing (fake emails luring you to reveal credentials), pretexting (inventing a scenario), baiting (leaving an infected USB to be found), and tailgating (following someone into a secure area).

Don't confuse it with these

TermWhat it actually is
Social engineeringManipulating people to reveal info or grant access
Threat agentThe actor/entity that carries out an attack
BotnetA network of compromised machines controlled remotely
Antivirus engineA defensive tool that detects and removes malware
The trap The question's tell is "through personal interactions." That's social engineering. A botnet is the tool, a threat agent is the person behind the attack, and an antivirus engine is a defense — none of them describe acquiring info by interacting with people.
