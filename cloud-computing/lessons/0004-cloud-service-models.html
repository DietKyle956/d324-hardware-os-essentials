Cloud Service Models: SaaS, IaaS, PaaS, HaaS

Four ways the cloud is delivered, from a finished app down to raw hardware.

The win

By the end of this lesson you'll know a vendor-managed application (like a picture editor) is SaaS (Q60), and where IaaS, PaaS, and HaaS sit relative to it.

The four models

ModelWhat the provider gives youExam tell
SaaSA finished, managed application"vendor-managed app", "edit pictures"
PaaSA platform to build/deploy your own apps"develop/deploy without managing servers"
IaaSInfrastructure — servers, storage, networking"rent servers/storage/networking"
HaaSPhysical hardware as a service"hardware"

Software as a Service (SaaS) is the most finished level: the vendor runs and manages the whole application, and you just use it. A vendor-managed cloud application that lets users edit pictures is SaaS.

Going down the stack, PaaS hands you a platform for building your own software, IaaS rents you the raw servers/storage/network, and HaaS provides the physical hardware itself.

The trap

The keyword is "vendor-managed application." A ready-to-use app you don't build or maintain = SaaS. If you're building on a platform, it's PaaS; if you're renting infrastructure, it's IaaS.
